## Top Stories

### NU7 Coinholder Vote — August 25

The biggest governance moment since Ironwood. Sean Bowe (Valar Group / Project Tachyon) called a coinholder vote starting **August 25**, snapshot **August 24 at 19:00 UTC**, closing approximately September 14. Five questions on the ballot:

1. **NSM issuance smoothing** — smooth emission curve vs. preserve halvings
2. **NSM reissuance start date** — when the new supply model begins
3. **Sprout deprecation timing** — sunset the oldest shielded pool
4. **Faster block times (ZIP-218)** — 75s → 25s (3× throughput)
5. **NU7 overall schedule** — target activation timeline

Minimum legitimacy threshold: 1,000,000 ZEC. Vizor and ZODL have both confirmed voting support. Poll questions received minor adjustments this week after coordination between Project Tachyon, Valar Group, Zcash Foundation, Shielded Labs, and ZODL. [Forum](https://forum.zcashcommunity.com/t/nu7-coinholder-vote/56912)

### Zcash Labs Launches

New independent go-to-market organization announced August 12 by Tom Howard (@thowar2). Focus: technical Zcash integrations for businesses and institutions. Their first supported project — **ZCASHTOCASH** — went live the same week: a peer-to-peer shielded ZEC offramp connecting to Venmo, Revolut, Cash App, Chime, Monzo, and Zelle across 100+ geographies.

Funding model: Zcash Labs fronts costs and seeks retroactive reimbursement from coinholders plus a 20% markup. Different from ZCG — more like venture-style bootstrapping for Zcash ecosystem projects. [Forum](https://forum.zcashcommunity.com/t/announcing-zcash-labs-a-zcash-go-to-market-company/56975)

### Ironwood Migration: 75% Complete

Orchard is down to 772K ZEC — from 3.8M at Ironwood's activation 19 days ago. Ironwood now holds 3.06M ZEC (~$1.5B), making it the largest shielded pool by a wide margin. At ~7,500 ZEC/hour, Orchard could empty within two weeks.

Supply verification now sits at approximately 95%: only the remaining Orchard balance is unauditable. When it fully drains, Zcash supply becomes near-100% verifiable for the first time since the Orchard bug disclosure. [ZecBlock Ironwood tracker](https://zecblock.com/ironwood)

### 37 Retroactive Grant Proposals — Record Batch, $9M Requested

The Q3 Coinholder-Directed Retroactive Grants Program closed submissions August 14 with **37 proposals** totaling **$9,014,383** requested — by far the largest cohort since the program launched. The 30-day review period runs through September 16, followed by a coinholder poll September 17–29.

Nine proposals cluster around Ironwood security work: Project Tachyon's formal verification, Taylor Hornby's Orchard bug bounty, two community-nominated "bonus grants" pushing Taylor's total to $1.5M, Valar Group's $1.2M Ironwood engineering claim (ebfull: "legendary... it probably wouldn't be live for at least another month or two" without them), a Zebra CVE bounty, five Zebra consensus-divergence vulnerabilities, and an external audit reimbursement. [37-proposal review thread](https://forum.zcashcommunity.com/t/30-day-review-period-coinholder-directed-retroactive-grants-program-q3/57056) · [Full list](https://forum.zcashcommunity.com/t/call-for-proposals-coinholder-directed-retroactive-grants-program-q3/56885)

Valar Group shipped Zakura 1.2.0 on August 14, removing redundant proof verification from block acceptance. Full block processing dropped from 2.5s to 0.32s — sharply reducing orphan rates. Zakura went from v1.0.0 introduction (Jul 15) to production-grade v1.2.0 in exactly one month. Currently 44 nodes (~14% of network). [Announcements](https://zakura.com/announcements/)

## Privacy Corner: Obfuscation vs Encryption

"Obfuscation and other passthrough methods are no match for modern AI." This line from @cypherpunk this week cuts to the core of why most "privacy" tools fail.

**Obfuscation** hides a needle in a haystack. Mixers shuffle UTXOs, tumblers add hops, ring signatures include decoys. The data is still *there* — it's just harder to find. With enough compute, pattern analysis, or machine learning, an attacker can un-shuffle what was shuffled. Every year the haystack gets easier to search.

**Encryption** destroys the haystack entirely. Zcash's zero-knowledge proofs don't hide your transaction among others — they mathematically prove a transaction is valid without revealing sender, receiver, or amount to *anyone*. There is no data to un-shuffle because the data was never exposed. No amount of AI, compute, or time changes that.

The distinction matters more each year. As chain analysis tools get smarter, obfuscation degrades. Encryption doesn't. Shield your ZEC — it's not hiding, it's math.
