## Top Stories

### Zakura Ships "Common" — 14x Faster Proving, No Network Upgrade Needed

The optimizations teased last week are out. On August 29, Zakura released Common, a set of cryptography and protocol libraries that deliver 14x faster proof generation on mobile, 5x faster on desktop, 21x faster Sinsemilla hashing, 1.5x faster trial decryption, and 4–8x faster zk-SNARK verification. These are pure library improvements — no consensus change required. Wallets and full nodes that adopt Common get the speedup immediately. Vizor has already confirmed integration; other wallets can follow by adding the relevant crates (e.g. `zakura-bellman` v1.0.1). Mert called it "an insane engineering feat." This is the largest single release of cryptographic optimizations in Zcash's history, and it landed one month after Ironwood activation. [Zakura announcement](https://x.com/ZakuraZcash/status/2093715267163758922) · [Forum](https://forum.zcashcommunity.com/t/zakura-common/57240)

### The Zcash ETF Is Live

ZCSH began trading on NYSE Arca on August 25 — the filing we covered last edition is now real. The fund launched with roughly 387,000 ZEC (~$313M AUM). Jane Street and Virtu are authorized participants; BNY administers. ZEC hit $855 on listing day — an eight-year high — and centralized exchange volume exceeded $1.2B in 24 hours, roughly 5x the prior month's daily average. Bankless noted the irony worth sitting with: "ZCSH holds its ZEC in transparent Coinbase custody wallets, so the ETF gives investors price exposure without touching the shielded tech that makes Zcash a privacy asset in the first place." More on this in Privacy Corner below. [Grayscale](https://www.thezcashetf.com/) · [Bankless](https://www.bankless.com/read/news/grayscales-zcash-etf-begins-trading-on-nyse-arca)

### NU7 Governance Week: The Sprout Debate

Both votes are now live — the coinholder poll opened August 25 and the ZCAP poll followed on August 27, both running through September 14. What made this week notable isn't the mechanics (covered last edition) but the debate that erupted around Question 3: Sprout deprecation.

Cypherpunk published their full NU7 recommendations: preserve halvings, begin fee recycling, don't deprecate Sprout yet, and leave block times at 75s. Sean Bowe publicly disagreed on Sprout, arguing it should be disabled immediately at NU7 — calling it technical debt, an unnecessary security risk, and inconsistent with Ironwood's circulating supply definition since Sprout isn't sealed like Orchard. He also noted that Sprout's remaining activity is almost entirely withdrawals, which is exactly what you'd expect before a shutdown. Zooko weighed in too. This is the most substantive public protocol debate Zcash has had in years, and it's playing out in the open while votes are being cast privately. [Cypherpunk recommendations](https://x.com/will__mcevoy/status/2092597465992122793) · [ebfull on Sprout](https://x.com/ebfull/status/2092624925823815974) · [ZCAP poll announcement](https://forum.zcashcommunity.com/t/zcap-poll-now-open-nu7-august-2026/57222)

## Privacy Corner: Price Exposure Is Not Privacy

*A practical note, prompted by this week's ETF launch.*

The Zcash ETF is a milestone — the first privacy-focused cryptocurrency accessible through a standard U.S. brokerage account. But it's worth being precise about what ZCSH does and doesn't give you.

ZCSH gives you price exposure to ZEC. Your shares sit in a brokerage account, tied to your name, social security number, and tax reporting. The fund's ZEC sits in Coinbase Custody's transparent wallets — every coin publicly visible on-chain, every movement traceable. If you hold ZCSH, Grayscale knows you hold it, your broker knows, Coinbase knows, and the IRS knows. That's the opposite of what Zcash was designed for.

The ETF is useful. It brings institutional capital, legitimacy, and liquidity. It lets retirement accounts get ZEC exposure. But it doesn't make you private. For that, you need self-custody and shielded transactions — the part of Zcash that doesn't show up in any brokerage statement. The ETF is the on-ramp to the idea. The wallet is the on-ramp to the reality.

*If the ETF convinced you that ZEC is worth owning, the next step is owning it for real: self-custody, shielded, sovereign.*
